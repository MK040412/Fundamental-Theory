TL;DR. Which synthetic demos make a vision-language-action model (VLA) act well after training?

  • Where. The loss appears only where the outcome is decided: the decision and the grasp.
  • Information. The decision follows what the model's representation carries. Putting the answer into the conditioning \(c\) takes it from 59% to 100%.
  • Coverage. An unresolved decision drives the robot into states the data never covered (58% vs 14% of the time). One round of data from those states did not beat the same amount of fresh demos.

1. What are we asking?

Which synthetic data raises inference performance after training?

Synthetic data \(D\) trains the policy \(\pi_D\), and we measure its performance \(J(\pi_D)\). First we define good data with equations. Then we test the definition on a two-basket task.

2. What should a deep learning reader know first?

A VLA is an image and text encoder plus a generative action head.

  • Encoder (System 2). A frozen vision-language model reads the camera image and the instruction and outputs tokens \(c\).
  • Action head (System 1). A flow-matching model (like a diffusion model) turns noise \(\varepsilon\) into the next 1.2 s of robot motion given \(c\).
  • Training. Plain imitation of demos from a scripted expert (the oracle) that sees the true simulator state.
  • Two kinds of mistakes. A wrong decision (which basket) and an imprecise motion (where the hand lands).
  • OOD. A state the trained policy reaches that never appears in the training data.

3. How does a VLA pick an action?

The VLM only guides through \(c\). The actual choice happens in the noise \(\varepsilon\).

$$\pi_\theta(a \mid h_t, \ell), \qquad c_t = g(h_t, \ell), \qquad a = \Phi(\varepsilon;\ o_t, c_t)$$

Figure 1. The noise picks the mode. (a) Noise \(p\) and a two-mode action distribution \(q\). (b) The path \(p_t\). (c) Learned vs target velocity. (d) Different noises end in different modes.

\(c\) depends only on \(h\) and \(\ell\), so it adds no information. System 2 can only extract what is already there.

4. Does the trained VLA pick the mode with the noise?

Yes. Without the answer in \(c\), \(\varepsilon\) picks one of two answers.

Figure 2. Figure 1, drawn from the trained VLA. One observation without answer information, 4000 noises pushed through the learned velocity field. The noises split between the left basket \(X_1^{(1)}\) and the right basket \(X_1^{(2)}\).

5. When does the loss occur?

Only at decision points and precision points.

$$J(\pi^*) - J(\pi_D) = \sum_t \mathbb{E}_{h_t \sim d_t^{\pi_D}}\big[r_D(h_t)\big], \qquad r_D(h) = V^{\pi^*}(h) - \mathbb{E}_{a \sim \pi_D(\cdot \mid h)}\big[Q^{\pi^*}(h, a)\big]$$

Figure 3. Loss is a spike, not a constant. \(r_D(h)\) is the mass of \(\pi_D\) outside the optimal set \(A^*(h)\). At every other step the loss is zero.

6. When does the loss occur in real rollouts?

At the decision in the first seconds, and at the grasp.

7. Where must the data be accurate?

Only where the outcome is decided.

8. Do the three cases appear in real rollouts?

Yes. A free choice is free. A wrong basket or an off-set palm is not.

11. Does the data cover where the policy goes?

We cannot know it at generation time, so we iterate.

$$\operatorname{supp}\, d^{\mathcal{A}(D)} \subseteq \operatorname{supp}\, \mu_D$$

Yes. The decision follows the information in the representation.

Yes. Resolved decision 40% vs unresolved decision about 22%.

15. Why does an unresolved decision hurt execution?

It leaves the data. 58% vs 14% of the time outside the training data.

Figure 14. No data there, nothing to imitate. Answer basket in the robot frame. Orange = training data, gray = visited states, red = visited but outside the data. Lift 38% when resolved vs 16% when unresolved.

16. Does putting the answer into \(c\) fix the decision?

Yes. 59% → 100%, and a flipped \(z\) sends it to the other basket.

Figure 15. System 2 extraction. Same data and steps. Only \(z\) is added to \(c\). The action head follows \(c\), so System 2 only has to extract the answer.

17. Does data from where the policy goes beat more data?

Not in this test. Fresh oracle data helped more.

Figure 16. One round of \(k\). Lift 38% → 52% with fresh data (\(p = 0.005\)) and 46% with data from the states the policy reached (\(p = 0.19\)). Here grasp precision is the bottleneck.

18. What is good synthetic data?

Data that precisely covers the answer fixed by information the learner can see, at the states the trained policy will visit, and above all where the outcome is decided.

19. What remains?

Repeat seeds and test coverage where the policy really leaves the data.

  • Seeds. Three training seeds per arm to tighten every interval.
  • Coverage on the unresolved task. Takeover rounds \(k = 1, 2\) where the policy is outside the data 58% of the time.
  • Precision data. More demos near the grasp to raise the 25 to 52% execution ceiling.
  • Self-correction. Compare the policy's retry rate with the oracle's own retry rate.
  • Real System 2. Let the VLM produce \(z\) instead of giving it.
